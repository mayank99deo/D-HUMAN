## In one minute

- **What:** a blog at `/blog` that both founders post to from a phone or laptop, with photos. It grows search traffic on the topics D-Human already owns: Indian food and calories, blood reports, preventive health, health tech, and D-Human's own features.
- **Reader side:** an index (one featured post, a list, five categories), post pages with a cover photo, byline with a photo and short bio, dates, reading time, and a body with headings, photos with captions, pull quotes, callouts and a "Try it" link into the app. Related posts at the end.
- **The way in:** one quiet "Blog" link in the landing page's footer, the same link in the guides' footer, and "Blog" beside the wordmark on blog pages. Nothing louder.
- **On brand with photos:** the page stays black and white. Recommended: photos go black and white too; app screenshots and charts keep their colour, because there colour carries meaning. Mayank decides.
- **Authoring, recommended:** a small editor inside the app at `/write`, behind the email-code sign-in you already have, open only to listed author emails. The health checklist is built into Publish and can't be skipped. Ship the reader side first, with the first posts loaded from the founders' Google Docs, so search starts working while the editor is built.
- **Not in v1:** scheduling, comments, tags, newsletter, translated pairs of posts.

## 1. The problem and who it's for

D-Human has one landing page and five guides. Guides answer one search each; they can't carry news, opinion, how-tos or the stories behind features. Competitors rank with hundreds of pages (STRATEGY §1). The founders want to write regularly without asking an engineer each time.

**Readers, and the moment they arrive:**

| Who | Moment | What they need from the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Priya, 40, regular runner in Indore | Searches "ferritin low runners" on her phone after a club run, on 4G | A clear answer near the top, sources she can check, and a calm "worth showing a doctor" line. Then, maybe, "Read my report". |
| Rohit, 28, starting from zero in a tier 3 town, reads Hinglish, ₹8,000 Android with Data Saver on | Searches "roti me kitni calorie hoti hai" | A page that loads fast without photos, words he understands, and "Try it in Fuel" in his language. |
| Arjun, 33, HYROX athlete in Mumbai, already a D-Human user | Taps "Blog" in the footer to see what's new | "Inside D-Human" posts: what Wraps show, how the stack is worked out. |
| A journalist or partner gym in Pune | Checking who's behind D-Human | Named authors with photos and real bios. |

**Authors:**

| Who | How they write |
| --- | --- |
| Mayank (CTO) | Mostly on a laptop, sometimes a phone. Comfortable with tools, but shouldn't need code to post. |
| The co-founder | On a phone between meetings. Takes food photos on that phone. Drafts may start in Google Docs or Notes and get pasted in. |

**Fit with the product's spine.** Posts teach the spine: test, read, food first, supplement only where a marker points, retest. Posts never sell a supplement, never scare, never diagnose. The blog doesn't touch the safety floor in `lib/domain/markers.ts`.

## 2. Reader side

### 2.1 Where it lives (proposal; the SEO lead decides)

| Page | Proposed URL |
| --- | --- |
| Index | `/blog` |
| Post | `/blog/<slug>` (lowercase English words and hyphens, no dates; STRATEGY §3) |
| Category | `/blog/category/<slug>` |
| Author | `/blog/authors/<slug>` |
| Older posts | `/blog/page/2` … (plain links, not infinite scroll, so crawlers and slow phones both cope) |
| Feed | `/blog/rss.xml` (cheap, and some readers and aggregators use it) |

The editor lives at `/write`, outside `/blog`. Add `/write` to `APP_PATHS` in `lib/seo.ts` so robots.txt disallows it, and give it noindex.

### 2.2 The ways in

1. **Landing page, last slide.** A "Blog" link in the footer. Proposed place: the right end of the wordmark's row (`Footer.tsx:14`), in small graphite text like the guide links, at least 44 px tall.
   - **Hard rule:** the last slide is sized to a 13rem footer (`app/page.tsx:66`, `Footer.tsx:6-8`). The link must not make the footer taller at `lg`. On laptops the wordmark's column has spare height beside the 3-line disclaimer; the wordmark row can grow to 44 px if the tagline's top margin shrinks to match. Measure at 1024×768, 1280×800 and 1440×900. If it can't fit, the fallback is the copyright line ("© 2026 D-Human · Blog").
   - This row becomes the home for About, Contact, Privacy and Terms later (STRATEGY Phase 1b), so design it as a links row with one item for now.
   - Show it in both site modes. Blog CTAs follow the mode, as the guides do (`CtaForm` in `Guide.tsx:39`).
   - Ship the link in the same deploy as the first live post, never before.
2. **Guides footer.** Add "Blog" after the five guide links (`Footer.tsx:17-31`).
3. **Blog header.** On blog pages the header shows the wordmark (to `/`) then "Blog" (to `/blog`) in small semibold text, then Sign in as today. One extra word, no new nav.
4. **Not in the app's tab bar.** The app stays focused on the person's own data. A link from Settings ("What's new on the blog") can come later.

### 2.3 Blog index

**Tone:** black, like the landing page.

**At 390 px, top to bottom:**

1. Header (64 px): wordmark, "Blog", Sign in.
2. Title block: H1 "The D-Human blog" (semiwide, `text-title`). Dek in graphite: "Food, blood tests and preventive health, for how India eats and trains. Written by the people building D-Human."
3. **Category row:** links, not filters, so each category has a crawlable page. All · Food and calories · Blood tests · Preventive health · Health tech · Inside D-Human. One row that scrolls sideways; the scroller must be `relative` (MEMORY gotcha "Scrolling chip rows"). The current one is inverted (white with black text) and has `aria-current="page"`. Each 44 px tall. Hide a category until it has a post.
4. **Featured post:** cover photo edge to edge at 3:2. Below it: category label (small, semibold), title (semiwide bold, about 1.75rem), dek in graphite (2–3 lines), byline (author photo 32 px, name, date, "6 min read"). The whole block is one link; the title is the accessible name.
5. **The list:** rows split by hairlines. Left: "Category · 18 Nov 2026", title (bold, 1.125rem), "6 min read". Right: an 88 px square thumbnail cropped from the photo's focal point. 12 posts a page. Under Data Saver (`Save-Data` header), rows show no thumbnails and the text takes the full width.
6. "Older posts" link when there's a page 2.
7. "Guides" strip: the five guides as text links (from `GUIDES`).
8. Closing Start free (open mode) or Join the waitlist (waitlist mode), then the footer.

**On laptops (from `lg`):** the category row sits in one line under the H1. The featured post splits the 12-column grid: photo on columns 1–7, text on 8–12, centred vertically. The list becomes a 3-column grid of cards: 3:2 photo, category, title, 2-line dek, byline. Hover underlines the title (only under `(hover: hover)`); no zoom-on-hover, no shadows, no rounded cards.

**Featured:** the post an author pins; if none is pinned, the newest. Only on page 1.

### 2.4 Category and author pages

- **Category page:** same list as the index, no featured post. H1 is the category name; a one-line intro the SEO lead writes (unique text keeps the page from being thin). Breadcrumb: D-Human / Blog / Food and calories.
- **Author page:** square photo, name, role ("Co-founder, D-Human"), bio (up to 200 characters), up to two profile links (LinkedIn, X), then their posts. Bios state only real roles and qualifications; nobody is called a doctor or dietitian unless they are one.

### 2.5 Post page

**Tone:** a black title band, then the body on white ("white pack, black ink"). Black text on white is the easier long read in daylight, and photos sit better on it. The guides already alternate black and white sections, so this is inside the system.

**At 390 px, top to bottom:**

1. Header.
2. **Black band:**
   - Breadcrumb: Blog / Food and calories (each a link, 44 px tall).
   - H1, semiwide bold, `text-balance`, `max-w-[20ch]`. Titles over 60 characters drop a size on phones (the same rule the verdict uses).
   - Dek (standfirst), one or two sentences, larger than body text.
   - Byline: author photo (40 px square with the logo's cut top-left corner, the notch the primary button uses), name (links to the author page), "18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read". When the author marked a meaningful update: "Updated 20 Nov 2026" on its own line.
   - "On this page", a closed `<details>`, only when the post has 3 or more H2s.
3. **Cover photo:** edge to edge, 3:2, loaded with priority (it's the likely largest paint). Caption and credit below in small graphite text.
4. **White band, the body:** 20 px side padding, body text 18 px with 1.6 line height, Archivo at normal width. Blocks in 2.6.
5. **Sources:** numbered, as the guides do (`SourcesAndMore`, `Guide.tsx:249`).
6. **Fixed lines (authors can't edit or remove them):** "Written by {Name} from the sources above. General information, not medical advice. If something here applies to you, it's worth asking a doctor." A post that mentions report reads also carries: "D-Human's report reads are AI-analysed. No doctor reviews them."
7. **Author box:** photo, name, role, bio, "More from {first name}".
8. **Share:** "Share" (the phone's own share sheet, so WhatsApp is one tap) and "Copy link" ("Link copied"). No third-party share buttons: they add weight and trackers.
9. **Black band:** "Try D-Human" with Start free (open mode) or Join the waitlist.
10. **Related posts:** up to 3. Same category first, newest first, then the newest from any category. With no other posts, show the guides instead.
11. Footer with guide links and Blog.

**On laptops:** the title band uses columns 1–9. The cover runs the full 1280 px grid. The body column is about 680 px (columns 4–9), with a sticky left rail (columns 1–3) holding the byline, "On this page" and Share. Images marked "wide" break out to columns 2–11.

### 2.6 Body blocks

Every block renders through our own components from structured content, never pasted HTML.

| Block | What it looks like | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paragraph | Body text; bold, italic, links (underlined) | Links are `http(s)` only. Links to app screens use the Try it block instead. |
| Heading | H2 (semiwide, about 1.5rem) and H3 (bold, 1.25rem) | No H4. H2s feed "On this page" and get anchor links. |
| List | Bullets as small black squares (like `Points`) or numbers | One level, no nesting. |
| Photo | Column width, or "wide" on laptops | Alt text required. Caption and credit optional. Colour rule in 2.7. |
| Pull quote | Large semiwide text (`text-lead`) under a 2 px black rule. No curly-quote graphic. | Quotes a line from this post or a named person who agreed. Never a health claim the body can't back. |
| Callout: Note | Hairline box, an "i" glyph in a square, bold title, text | For asides and tips. |
| Callout: Worth showing a doctor | Hairline box, the "!" glyph (the brand's error mark), fixed title "Worth showing a doctor" | Says when to see a doctor. Never says what the reader has. |
| Callout: Key number | A large number (`display-num`), one line on what it means, and a required source | A number can't be saved without picking a source from the post's Sources. |
| Try it | A black box inside the white body, one line of text and the cut-corner primary button | Picks a destination from a fixed list (below). The author can edit the line (up to 90 characters), not the button. |
| Table | Simple table like the guides' `DataTable`, up to 4 columns and 12 rows, scrolls sideways inside its own box | Caption required; a source is required when it holds numbers. Hardest block to edit on a phone; can slip to v1.1. |

**Try it destinations and default copy:**

| Destination | Line | Button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fuel (`/fuel`) | Log your next meal by photo, voice or text, in your language. | Try it in Fuel |
| Upload (`/upload`) | Upload a blood report you already have. AI reads it for how you train. | Read my report |
| Stack (`/stack`) | See what your markers and meals point to, food first. | See my stack |
| Wraps (`/wraps`) | Your week of meals, played back as a story. | See my wrap |
| Start free (`/signin`) | AI reads your blood report and counts the calories and protein in your meals. | Start free |
| A guide | The guide's `blurb` from `lib/seo.ts` | Read the guide |

Waitlist mode turns every app destination into "Join the waitlist" (to `/#join`).

**Gap found while specifying Try it:** a signed-out reader who taps "Try it in Fuel" hits `/fuel`, is sent to `/signin`, and after the code lands on `/home` or `/onboarding`, never Fuel (`app/api/auth/verify/route.ts:30`). Sign-in should return people to the screen they tapped, after onboarding for new people. Accept only our own app paths, so it can't become an open redirect. Owner: staff engineer.

### 2.7 Photos on a black-and-white brand

The brand has no colour except blood status colours, always beside a glyph. A photo-heavy blog needs a rule, or phone photos in every white balance will break the look.

- **Recommended (decision 1): photos in black and white.** Every photo shows in monochrome by default. Screenshots of D-Human and charts keep their colour: there, colour carries meaning (marker status), and that colour already belongs to the system. The author ticks "This is a screenshot or chart" to keep colour. Mixed phone photos then read as one set, poor lighting hides, and the page looks like D-Human, not a recipe site. The original is kept, so the rule can change later.
- **The alternative: colour photos inside a black-and-white frame.** Food looks more appetising, which is Zomato's strength. But each post then brings its own colours, and the brand's rule that the only colour comes from blood stops holding on these pages.
- **Either way:** no coloured overlays, gradients, tinted tags or coloured buttons. Category labels are text. The UI around photos stays black, white and grey.
- **Crops come from one photo and one tap.** The author taps the most important part of the photo (the focal point). Code crops 3:2 for covers and cards, 1:1 for list thumbnails, and 1.91:1 for the share image, all around that point. No crop tool to learn.
- **A post with no cover photo** gets a drawn cover: contour lines in the style of the hero and "Your contour", seeded by the post's slug so each post's drawing is its own and never changes. Static SVG, rounded coordinates (MEMORY gotcha "SVG and hydration"), decorative (`aria-hidden`). Owner: graphic designer. The same drawing, with the title and wordmark, makes the share image.
- **An author with no photo** gets a monogram: initials, semiwide, on the raised black (`lift`), with the notch.

### 2.8 Hindi and Hinglish

- Each post has a language: English, Hindi or Hinglish. The page sets `lang` (`en-IN`, `hi`, and `hi-Latn` for Hinglish, following `langTag` in the meal flow). Titles in lists carry their own `lang`.
- **Devanagari:** use the phone's own font, as the landing does, not the 726 KB Anek file (MEMORY Snapshot and Open items). Archivo's wide axis doesn't apply there. Give Devanagari headings at least 1.25 line height so the vowel marks above the line aren't clipped (the site's titles run at 1).
- The page chrome stays English, as the app's labels do. Post-level labels follow the post: "6 मिनट" and "18 नवंबर 2026" on a Hindi post.
- Slugs stay in English letters (STRATEGY §3). A Hindi title gets an English address the author writes.
- The fixed "not medical advice" line in Hindi and Hinglish needs a native speaker's check; it joins the open item "Hindi and Hinglish versions of the fixed doctor wording" in `MEMORY.md`. Until then Hindi posts carry the English line.
- One post, one language. Linked translations of the same post (with hreflang) come later, with the SEO lead.

### 2.9 Slow phones and low data

Targets to measure on a production build, not promises:

- Index first load at 390 px under 300 KB; post cover under 120 KB at 390 px.
- Pages are static HTML. Only Share, Copy link and Start free need script.
- Only the post cover (or the featured photo on the index) loads with priority; everything else loads lazily with a width and height set, so nothing jumps (CLS 0).
- The space for a photo is held by a flat grey box, not a blurry preview.
- Data Saver drops list thumbnails.
- No video, carousels, web fonts beyond Archivo, or third-party embeds in v1.
- No offline reading in v1. Without a connection the browser shows its own offline page.

### 2.10 Accessibility

One H1 per page. Headings in order. Alt text on every photo. Captions are `<figcaption>`. Callout glyphs have text equivalents ("Note:", "Worth showing a doctor:"). Tap targets at least 44 px. Grey text keeps the contrast the theme already sets (graphite on black, smoke on white). Reduced motion: nothing on the blog moves anyway.

## 3. Author side

### 3.1 Three ways to write, compared

| | A. Editor in the app at `/write` | B. Import from Google Docs or Notion | C. Hosted CMS (Sanity, Contentful; or Ghost, WordPress) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effort (my estimate) | Most: about 2–3 weeks for one engineer for v1 | Medium: importer, format rules, image copying | Medium: content model, preview mode, publish hooks |
| New monthly cost | None. Photos need public storage (Supabase Storage or Vercel Blob); check free-tier limits | Free tiers; an API token to keep secret | Free tiers exist (Sanity's per third-party sources; check sanity.io/pricing); seats and features cost as you grow. Hosted Ghost or WordPress are paid. |
| Writing on a phone | Designed by us for 390 px: one block at a time | Best: apps they already know | Built for desktop first (my inference); usable, cramped |
| Photos | Upload, focal point, alt text, black-and-white rule, location data removed, all ours | Must be copied to our storage at import. Notion's file links expire after one hour, so static pages that point at them break. | Strong (crops, alt text) |
| Health checklist | Built into Publish; can't be skipped | Only at import, away from where they write | Possible with custom validation, within the vendor's limits |
| Preview | The real page | Needs an import first | Needs a preview mode we build |
| Fit with the SEO lead's URLs and structured data | Full control; same data feeds sitemap and JSON-LD | Full control after import | Full control if headless. Ghost or WordPress would sit on another stack or subdomain: off brand, and splits search signals. |
| Sign-in | The email code they already use | Notion or Google accounts plus our import step | A new login for both |
| Main risk | We own an editor; scope creep | Formatting surprises; two sources of truth; a doc shared too widely leaks a draft | A new vendor; content outside our database; plan changes |

**Recommendation: A, staged.**

- **Stage 0 (about a week):** build the reader side. The founders write the first 2–3 posts in Google Docs; the crew loads them as post records (not hand-built pages), so the editor later opens them like any other post. Search starts indexing while the editor is built. Every Stage 0 post goes through the checklist in section 4, read aloud by the orchestrator and ticked by the author.
- **Stage 1:** `/write` v1: posts list, editor, photos, details, preview, publish, update, take down, author profiles, the checklist and automatic checks.
- **Stage 2, if wanted:** scheduling, preview links for an outside reviewer, an optional AI copy check, version history.

Why A: the health checklist is the one thing that must never be skipped, and only A puts it in the Publish button. It also needs no new vendor, no new login and no monthly bill, and it uses the sign-in, database and design system already built. Pasting from Google Docs (3.4) gives back most of B's comfort.

### 3.2 Who can write

- Authors are listed by email in a small `authors` table: name, role, bio, photo, links. Mayank adds the co-founder; adding a guest later is one row.
- `/write` uses the normal email-code sign-in. Every write request checks the author list on the server, not just the page.
- Someone signed in but not listed sees: "This area is for D-Human's writers." with a link home. It never says which emails are listed.
- An author doesn't need to finish the app's onboarding to use `/write`.

### 3.3 Posts list (`/write`)

- Tabs: **Drafts**, **Published**, **Taken down**.
- Each row: title (or "Untitled"), category, "Edited 10:42 by Mayank", and a status tag in text ("Draft", "Live", "Changes not live", "Ready for a read").
- Primary button (cut corner): **New post**.
- Empty: "No posts yet. Your first one starts here." with New post.

### 3.4 The editor

**On a phone,** top to bottom:

- **Top bar:** Back, status ("Draft · Saved 10:42"), Preview, Publish. Publish stays enabled; it opens the checklist, which explains what's missing.
- **Cover photo:** "Add a cover photo" (camera or gallery).
- **Title:** large field, counter from 70 characters, hard stop at 100. "Shorter titles read better on phones and in search."
- **Dek:** optional, up to 200 characters. "One or two sentences on what the reader gets."
- **Body:** a column of blocks. A "+" between blocks and at the end opens: Paragraph, Heading, List, Photo, Pull quote, Note, Worth showing a doctor, Key number, Try it, Table.
  - Each block has Move up, Move down and Delete in a small menu. Buttons, not drag, because they work on every phone and with a screen reader.
  - Selecting text in a paragraph shows Bold, Italic, Link. The link box takes a URL or offers D-Human pages (the guides and other posts).
  - **Pasting from Google Docs, Notes or WhatsApp** keeps headings, bold, italic, links and lists, and drops fonts, colours and sizes. Pasted photos upload as Photo blocks and ask for alt text.
- **Under the body:** word count and reading time; **Sources** (each: name, optional link); **Details**.

**On a laptop:** the same column, centred, with Details open in a right-hand panel.

**Saving:** every change saves after a short pause. "Saving…", then "Saved 10:42".

### 3.5 Photos

1. Pick or take a photo. The browser shrinks it before upload, as meal photos already are (`components/platform/shrink.ts`), so it works on slow networks and stays under the host's request limit.
2. Upload progress on the block: "Uploading 60%". Location and camera data are removed before the photo is stored (phone photos carry GPS).
3. "Tap the most important part of the photo." The 3:2, square and share crops preview under it.
4. **Alt text, required:** "Describe the photo for someone who can't see it."
5. Caption and credit, optional.
6. Two ticks:
   - "I took this photo, or I have the right to use it." Credit becomes required when it isn't their own.
   - "This is a screenshot or chart (keep colour)". Only with decision 1's monochrome rule.
7. A photo under 1200 px wide gets a warning: "This photo is small and may look soft on laptops."

### 3.6 Details

| Field | Rule | Copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | One, required | "Where this post lives on the blog." |
| Language | English, Hindi or Hinglish | "The language the post is written in." |
| Author | The signed-in author; can pick the other | "Who wrote it. Their photo and bio show on the post." |
| Web address | Suggested from a Latin-letter title; lowercase letters, numbers, hyphens | Shows `d-human.fit/blog/` before the field. Hindi titles: "Write the address in English letters, for example roti-calories." |
| Search title | Defaults to the title; counter to 60 | Over 60: "Search engines will cut this. Write a shorter search title." |
| Search description | Required; counter to 155 | "What someone sees under the title in Google." |
| Share image | Defaults to the cover's 1.91:1 crop, or the drawn cover | "What shows when the link is shared on WhatsApp." |

### 3.7 Preview

- Opens the real post page with a top strip: "Preview. Not public." and a Phone / Laptop switch on laptops.
- It's only visible to signed-in authors and is never indexed.
- Stage 2: "Share a preview link" for an outside reviewer, valid 7 days, noindex.

### 3.8 Ready for a read (review)

- **Recommended (decision 3):** posts in Food and calories, Blood tests and Preventive health need the other founder's "Looks good" before Publish. Health tech and Inside D-Human can go out solo.
- The author taps "Ask {name} for a read"; the other founder gets an email (Resend is in place) with a link to the preview. They choose "Looks good" or "Needs changes" with a note.
- This is an internal check. The page never shows it as a review. A dated "Reviewed by" box appears only when a real doctor or dietitian reviews a post under their own name (STRATEGY §7).

### 3.9 Publish

1. Publish opens a sheet with the **automatic checks** (4.2), then the **checklist** (4.1). Blocking problems are listed first, each with the "!" glyph and a "Show me" that scrolls to the place.
2. When every check passes and every box is ticked: **Publish now**.
3. **Success:** "Live at d-human.fit/blog/roti-calories." with Copy link, Share and View post. The page is live within a minute.
4. **Failure:** "Couldn't publish. Your post is saved. Try again." Nothing half-publishes.

### 3.10 Edit, update, take down, delete

- **Editing a live post** works on a copy. Banner: "Changes aren't live yet." Buttons: Update post, Discard changes.
- **Update post** re-runs the automatic checks. The checklist is ticked again only when the body changed. A switch: "This is a meaningful update (shows 'Updated' with today's date)". Off for typo fixes, so the date stays honest.
- **Changing the address of a live post:** "Old links will send readers to the new address." The old slug is kept and redirects.
- **Take down:** "Take this post down? Its link will show 'This post isn't available', and search engines will drop it over time. You can put it back later." The post moves to Taken down. Putting it back restores the same address.
- **Delete:** drafts never published can be deleted ("Delete this draft? This can't be undone."). A taken-down post can be deleted for good only from Taken down, with the same warning; its address then answers "gone" (status per the SEO lead).
- **Scheduling: not in v1 (decision 4).** With two authors and a modest cadence, "Publish now" plus a reminder covers it. Scheduling adds a timed job, time-zone handling, and "why isn't it live?" moments. Add it in Stage 2 if they want a fixed day and time.

### 3.11 Author profile

In `/write`, "Your profile": photo (square, with the notch; focal point as for posts), name, role, bio (up to 200 characters), up to two links. Copy under the bio: "Say only what's true. Don't list a qualification you don't hold."

### 3.12 Saving, conflicts and connection loss

| Situation | What happens | Copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offline while writing | Changes are kept on the device and saved when the connection returns | "Offline. Your changes are kept on this phone and will save when you're back." |
| Save failed (server) | Retries; the device copy stays | "Couldn't save. Trying again…" then "Saved 10:44" |
| Session expired | The device copy stays; sign in and carry on | "You've been signed out. Sign in again; nothing you wrote is lost." |
| The other founder edits the same post | A soft lock, then a choice | Opening: "{Name} is editing this post (opened 2 min ago). Open anyway?" On a clash: "{Name} changed this post at 10:42. Keep yours, or load theirs?" |
| Photo upload failed | The block stays, with Retry | "Photo didn't upload. Retry." |
| Photo type not supported (for example an iPhone HEIC file on a laptop) | No upload | "This photo type isn't supported. Save it as JPEG and try again." |
| Address taken | Suggestion | "Another post uses this address. Try roti-calories-2, or reword it." |

## 4. Health content guardrails

These are built into Publish and Update. They don't replace a doctor's review; they stop the mistakes the house rules already forbid.

### 4.1 The checklist (all boxes ticked to publish)

Title on the sheet: "Before this goes live". Each box, as written:

1. **No diagnosis, no cures.** "The post talks about markers, ranges and risk. It never tells a reader they have a condition, and never says food or a supplement cures, treats, reverses or prevents a disease."
2. **Every number has a source.** "Each health number names its source in Sources: ICMR-NIN, a peer-reviewed paper, or a public dataset. Examples are labelled 'illustrative'."
3. **No 'doctor-reviewed'.** "Nothing says this post or D-Human's reads are reviewed by a doctor. Reads are called AI."
4. **No brand names.** "No supplement or lab brands, and no affiliate links."
5. **When to see a doctor.** "Anything about symptoms, medicines, pregnancy, kidneys or children points the reader to a doctor. No doses for medicines."
6. **App facts are current.** "Features match the app today. No prices or plan limits: they change. Link to the app instead."
7. **People and data.** "No real person's report, name or health details, unless it's your own and you're happy to share it. Anyone recognisable in a photo agreed to be in it. AI-made images say so in the caption."
8. **The fixed line.** "I've read the line every post ends with: 'General information, not medical advice.'"

Each publish stores who ticked what, when, and the automatic check results, against that version of the post.

**Optional, Stage 2:** "Check with AI". A low-cost model reads the draft against these rules and lists sentences to look at. It only advises; the author decides. The AI efficiency lead prices it before it ships.

### 4.3 What's fixed on every post

- The closing line in 2.5, step 6, plus the AI line when reads are mentioned.
- The author's real name and role. No "D-Human Health Team" byline that hides who wrote it.

## 6. Build notes for the engineers (not code)

- **Data:** posts (with a draft copy and a live copy), old slugs, authors, images (storage key, size, focal point, alt, caption, credit, keep-colour), and publish records (checklist ticks and check results by version). Categories are a fixed list in code, like `GUIDES`, so the SEO lead owns names and slugs. New tables have RLS on and migrations safe to run twice (MEMORY gotchas).
- **Images:** public storage, kept apart from health data. Never in `report_files`. Type checked by first bytes, as uploads are now. Location data stripped.
- **Content format:** structured blocks rendered by our components. No stored HTML, no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` for post bodies. Links `http(s)` only.
- **Pages:** static, refreshed when a post is published, updated or taken down. Read `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` on caching before building (Next 16 with Cache Components).
- **SEO lead owns:** URLs, title pattern, BlogPosting, Person and BreadcrumbList data, the sitemap entries (with `lastmod` from the meaningful-update date), the feed, pagination, status codes for removed posts, category intros.
- **QA:** the author check on every write route, upload abuse, link schemes, the preview's privacy, and that the footer link keeps the last slide on one screen.

## 7. How we'll know it works

- **Cadence:** posts a month, by author. The founders set the target.
- **Search:** Search Console impressions, clicks and position per post and per category, from week 4.
- **Reading:** visits per post (Vercel Web Analytics, already on).
- **Into the app:** Try it and Start free clicks from posts, and sign-ups that started on a post. This needs STRATEGY §8's first-landing-page proposal, after its privacy check.

## 8. Out of scope for v1

Comments; likes; tags (thin tag pages hurt search); a newsletter or "email me new posts"; scheduling; co-authored bylines; linked Hindi and English versions of one post; video and third-party embeds; offline reading; the blog inside the app's tab bar; guest authors beyond adding a row; analytics beyond what's already on.

## 9. Decisions for Mayank

1. **Photos:** black and white with screenshots and charts in colour (recommended), or colour photos inside the black-and-white frame.
2. **Authoring:** the in-app editor at `/write`, staged with the first posts loaded from Google Docs (recommended); or a hosted CMS; or Docs or Notion import.
3. **Review rule:** health-category posts need the other founder's "Looks good" before Publish (recommended), or either founder publishes alone.
4. **Scheduling:** leave out of v1 (recommended), or build it now.
5. **Authors' public details:** names, roles, photos and bios for both founders. Health sites do better with named people (STRATEGY §10.4).
6. **AI-assisted drafting:** may posts be drafted with AI help? If yes, recommended: allowed, with a human author who checks every fact and claim, and no "AI-generated" byline.
7. **Footer link before the trust pages:** ship "Blog" now in the new footer links row, with About, Privacy and Terms joining it when they exist.
